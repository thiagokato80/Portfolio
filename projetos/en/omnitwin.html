OmniTwin

Digital twins for industrial operations resilience

A graph-based discrete-event simulation platform: you model the operation visually, the engine runs failures, queues and constraints over time, and the result shows where the chain breaks before it actually breaks.

Digital Twin Simulation SimPy
3 node types: asset, process and stock
4 families of output KPI

The problem

Supply chain planning is usually done with averages. Average cycle time, average lead time, average availability. The problem is that operations do not break on average — they break when the machine fails during the week the supplier was late and the production mix changed.

Questions like "what happens if my main supplier is ten days late" have no answer in a spreadsheet of averages. They require running the operation with the failures inside it.

The solution

The architecture follows a decoupled simulation pattern: the Python calculation engine is entirely independent of the modelling interface, and the two communicate through strict JSON schemas validated by Pydantic. Replacing the interface does not touch the engine; changing the engine does not break a saved model.

  • Visual graph modelling — asset, process and stock nodes, connected on screen, with no simulation code to write.
  • Discrete-event engine in SimPy, with stochastic failure by MTBF/MTTR, a setup matrix for SKU changeovers, and bill-of-materials validation on every run.
  • Topology analysis with NetworkX for critical path and graph validation before simulating.
  • Stress testing — multiple simulations varying one parameter, such as a supplier delay.
  • Live telemetry over MQTT, with an explicit fallback rule: a parameter out of range or unavailable reverts to the model's stochastic value.

Every state change logs a timestamp from the simulation clock, not the wall clock — which is what makes root-cause analysis possible after the run.

Outcome

The output is not a single number but the behaviour of the operation over time: stock level series, a Gantt of failure and repair per asset, identification of the node that most constrained flow, and the financial impact of unmet demand.

That changes the question the team can ask — from "what is our capacity" to "where does this operation break first, and what does it cost".
