Condominium Procurement

From purchase request to payment, for property management companies

A platform that digitises the condominium purchasing cycle: opening a quotation, collecting bids, comparison map, line-by-line award and payment confirmation — with a configurable approval workflow.

Procurement Workflow Supabase
5 access profiles
3 levels of hierarchical catalogue
2 comparison map export formats

The problem

Buying for a condominium is a process with many hands and no system: the building manager requests, the service desk scopes it, the buyer collects quotes by email, someone consolidates the bids into a spreadsheet, and finance pays. Every step lives somewhere different.

The result is predictable — nobody knows the status of a purchase without asking someone, comparing suppliers becomes manual spreadsheet work, and the record of who approved what gets lost in an inbox.

The solution

A single flow, from quotation opening to payment confirmation, with the state of the process visible at any moment.

  • Kanban by phase, with drag between states, to see every process at once.
  • Comparison map with one column per supplier and cells coloured by status — best price, outlier, disqualified — exportable to Excel and PDF, with automatic scaling for many suppliers.
  • Line-by-line award: the winner of a quotation need not be the same for every item.
  • Three bid channels: market quotation, marketplace import via CSV/XLSX, and online purchase.
  • SLA alerts via pg_cron, with daily deduplication so the alert does not become noise.
  • Duplicate supplier merging and company-registration checks.

One product decision worth recording: when sending a quotation to a supplier, the platform composes the email and the buyer sends it from their own address. The system never acts as the sender — the supplier relationship stays with the person buying.

Outcome

The purchasing process stopped living in inboxes and loose spreadsheets and gained state, an owner and a history. Comparing suppliers became a screen rather than a consolidation exercise.
