Detective AI

Assessing AI competency through investigative simulation

A gamified platform where the candidate investigates a case by interrogating suspects who are AI personas. What is assessed is not the right answer, but how the person conducts the interrogation — prompt engineering, deductive reasoning and emotional control under pressure.

Assessment Gamification BYOK
4 interchangeable LLM providers
2 levels of data isolation

The problem

A CV lists "prompt engineering" and "critical thinking". There is no honest way to validate that in a multiple-choice test — the nuance of negotiating with an evasive model does not fit into options.

And training newcomers directly in the production system, on real data, trades one risk for another.

The solution

An investigation case as the assessment environment. The candidate interrogates LLM-driven suspects who respond in real time according to personality and context — not from a fixed script — and who remember what was said, can lie, and can contradict themselves.

  • Prompt engineering genuinely under test — getting around an alibi requires structuring commands that expose logical gaps; a verbose model requires persistence.
  • Stress meter per persona: accusing without evidence increases resistance. Knowing when to press and when to calm is part of the score.
  • Forensic reasoning — choosing the right tool for each piece of evidence and cross-referencing facts, not just asking.
  • Final accusation screen, requiring a synthesis of motive and method from what was collected.
  • Custom cases in JSON, including clues flagged as red herrings — the company writes its own mystery, such as an internal leak for compliance training.

The architecture is bring-your-own-key, at two levels: a corporate key with a no-training guarantee, or fully local execution via Ollama, with no data leaving the infrastructure. Switching providers also allows comparing how the same person handles different reasoning models.

Outcome

Assessment came to rest on behaviour observed under pressure rather than on a claim in a CV. As a training sandbox, getting the prompt wrong stopped costing a client.

The lie detector and the visual stress feedback work as immediate mentoring: the person sees the effect of their own approach in the moment it happens.
